HOCKEY SHOT TRACKER - TRYING IT AT HOME
=======================================

The app runs on a computer (Mac or Windows). You use it from a web page,
on the computer or on your phone, as long as both are on the same Wi-Fi.

To use it from your phone anywhere, without the computer, see
"PUT IT ON YOUR PHONE.txt" instead.


1. INSTALL PYTHON (ONCE)
------------------------
Go to https://www.python.org/downloads/ and install the latest version.
On Windows, tick "Add python.exe to PATH" on the installer's first screen.
(The starter below checks for it and opens that page if it's missing.)


2. START THE APP
----------------
Mac:     double-click "Start Hockey App (Mac).command"
Windows: double-click "Start Hockey App (Windows).bat"

The first time takes a few minutes while it sets itself up. After that it
starts in seconds. A window opens showing two addresses, and the app opens
in your browser. KEEP THAT WINDOW OPEN while you use the app; closing it
stops the app.

If the computer won't let it open:
- Mac, "cannot be opened" or "could not verify": open System Settings >
  Privacy & Security, scroll down, click "Open Anyway". Or open the
  Terminal app, type  bash  and a space, drag the .command file into the
  window, and press Return.
- Windows, "Windows protected your PC": click "More info", then "Run anyway".
- If it asks whether to allow network connections, click Allow. That is what
  lets your phone reach it.


3. FILM
-------
These matter more than anything else for accuracy:

- Record normal video at 60 fps: Settings > Camera > Record Video >
  1080p HD at 60 fps. (1080p analyses much faster than 4K. 30 fps is not
  accurate enough to trust.)
- Keep the phone still: prop it on a chair, a bag or a tripod.
- Put it about chest height, off to the side at about 45 degrees from the
  shooting lane, close enough that the net fills about a third of the
  screen width. The whole goal must be in view, including where the posts
  meet the ground.
- Measure how far the shooting spot is from the goal line, in feet.
- If your net isn't a regulation 6 ft x 4 ft (72 x 48 in) one, measure its
  opening: inside width, and height from the ground to the underside of the
  crossbar. Every mark's height depends on it.
- Keep the pile of spare pucks out of the puck's path, one shooter at a
  time, and a couple of seconds between shots. A clip can hold many shots.


4. ANALYSE
----------
- From your phone: open Safari and type in the "On your phone" address
  shown in the window (it looks like http://192.168.1.23:8000). Tap
  "Choose clips" and pick your videos.
- Or from the computer: AirDrop or copy the videos over and drag them onto
  the page.
- Fill in the shooting distance, the lens you filmed with (0.5x or 1x),
  where the phone was (on the ground, or raised on a chair or tripod),
  the net's opening in inches, and what you were aiming at. The page
  remembers these for next time. If the phone was on the ground, say so:
  from there a far net is too small to show how high the phone was, and
  on the slow-motion clips that fixed speeds that read up to 20% fast.
- If the notes say the bottom few inches of the posts looked hidden (grass,
  seen from low down), that's normal from the ground; the app allows for
  it. If they say the outline fits a different size of net, check the size
  you entered.
- Press Analyze. A 30-second clip at 1080p takes a minute or two; 4K
  takes several.

You get each shot's speed, where it went (marks on a picture of the net),
whether it hit the target, and the video with the puck's path drawn on it:
each shot gets a label like "#3 ✓ 47 mph" where it hit (✓ on net, ✗ missed).
Only the puck is drawn, not the shooter.
If the app can't find the net, it asks you to tap its four corners.

"Save video with shots" (under the video) makes a copy of the clip with all
of that drawn in, which plays on any phone. On the iPhone it lands in
Downloads (Files app); from there Share > Save Video puts it in Photos.


5. HOW MUCH TO TRUST IT
-----------------------
Measured on real backyard clips so far:
- 60 fps: most shots within about 3% on speed and 2-3 inches on the mark;
  now and then one is off by up to about 7% on speed. The two shots timed
  by hand read 47.0 and 50.4 mph against 47 and 50.
- Slow motion: the most accurate. Pick the lens in the form, because slow
  motion doesn't record it, and say the phone was on the ground if it
  was. The two newest slow-motion shots then read 54.9 and 50.5 mph
  against 55.4 and 51.8 worked out a second way (from the house and
  fence in the background).
- 30 fps: not accurate enough to trust.
It has only been tested in one backyard with one shooter so far. Clips
where it gets something wrong are the most useful thing to send back.


6. GOOD TO KNOW
---------------
- Sessions are saved in the "sessions" folder next to these files and come
  back each time you start the app. To keep your history when you get a
  newer version, copy that folder into the new one.
- The phone can't open the address? Check both are on the same Wi-Fi (not
  a guest network), and that you allowed network access when asked.
- The video doesn't play on a Windows computer? The browser can't play the
  iPhone's video format there. The speeds and marks are still right, and
  it plays on the phone and on a Mac. Or set the iPhone to
  Settings > Camera > Formats > Most Compatible.
